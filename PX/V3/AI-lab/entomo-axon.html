the challenge today

no intelligent core governs the building blocks of work

knowledge, skills, abilities, tasks, and performance each live in their own system, with nothing above them to connect what they mean.

knowledgeburied in an LMS catalog three reorgs old
skillsself-typed into profiles, never verified
abilitiesobserved in interviews, filed, forgotten
tasksa job description nobody has reopened
performancerunning in a separate tool entirely

so goals get set that don't match the role. learning gets assigned that doesn't serve the goal. reviews start from memory. and bolting an AI chatbot on top doesn't fix it: with nothing connecting the pieces, the chatbot guesses too.

why connection beats collection

intelligence lives in the connections, not the parts

a directory of skills can't tell you who's ready, and a library of courses can't tell you what to learn next. the answers only exist in how the pieces connect.

one language for work

when roles, goals, skills, and learning share one model, "ready", "gap", and "performing" mean the same thing in every process. workforce researchers call this the foundation without which nothing skills-based works.

processes feed each other

kept apart, performance data can't shape development, and skills can't shape goals, so both underperform. connected once, every cycle strengthens the next instead of starting over.

AI needs structure, or it guesses

language models are fluent, not grounded. give them a governed graph of how your work actually connects, and they answer from your truth, reason across relationships, and show their working.

work moves too fast for documents

employers expect roughly two in five core skills to change within five years. a framework written as a document is wrong within quarters. a model that lives updates itself.

meet axon

four building blocks, connected into one living model

axon is entomo's model of work, built from fifteen years of running HR processes: what a role must know, what its people can do, what they bring, and what the work demands, interlinked for every role in your organization.

Kknowledge

what the role must know: domain expertise, regulation, frameworks, context.

Sskills

what the person can do: practical, trainable capability, with proficiency levels.

Aabilities

what the person brings: problem-solving, leadership, adaptability, judgment.

Ttasks

what the role actually demands, activity by activity, tied to real KPIs.

grounded in global skills frameworks including ESCO, SFIA, and skillsfuture, and benchmarked against a live market graph of 80M+ resumes and 12M job descriptions, refreshed monthly.

the difference is grounding: axon answers from certified definitions of your work, cites what it used, and stops to ask a human when confidence drops.
