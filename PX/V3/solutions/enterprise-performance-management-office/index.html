the challenge today

most of your workforce today seems disconnected from the company's strategy.

the strategy group sets direction. human resources runs the review cycle. between them sit different cadences, different owners, and KPIs nobody has the capacity to challenge.

the typical structure
strategy group
✗ different cycle, different cadence
human resources
✗ goals cascade once a year, unchallenged
organization units → individual KPIs

strategy and performance never quite meet: the plan changes mid-year, the scorecards don't, and by review season the two describe different companies.

with an enterprise performance management office
strategy group
↓ one cadence, held by one office
enterprise performance management office
↓ continuous KPI alignment, challenged and current
organization units → individual KPIs

an agile structure between strategy and HR that keeps alignment continuous: personalized incentives, workforce analytics, and the standing capacity to challenge every KPI.

before you stand one up

is this consulting or software?

Both, deliberately. The advisory practice designs the office, audits your KPIs, and trains the team; the platform gives the office its instruments: scorecards, cascade, analytics, nudges. Software alone leaves the structure problem unsolved; advice alone doesn't operationalize.

we're not ready for a full office. can we start smaller?

Yes. The four stages are sequential on purpose: many clients begin with the KPI audit and company-wide goal alignment, then formalize the office once the cadence proves itself. One media-sector client was live in 45 days.

how is this different from the strategic performance management office?

The enterprise office is the bridge between the strategy group and HR, built to keep KPIs aligned to strategy continuously. The strategic office runs the performance process itself, cycle by cycle. Many enterprises grow into both. see the strategic performance management office →

what does the office run on day to day?

Balanced scorecards cascaded from executive to entity level, real-time dashboards, and the nudge engine carrying insight to whoever owns the metric. The same platform Forrester studied: $26.8M in benefits and 103% ROI over three years at a government entity.
